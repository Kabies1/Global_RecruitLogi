A guide for international nurses

How to Become a USRN: A Step-by-Step Guide

Explore the typical stages for internationally educated nurses considering a U.S. registered nurse opportunity, from the first application conversation through licensure and starting work.

The Global Recruitment & Logistics Healthcare Professionals process · A conversation with Carol Musiimenta and Bridget Wills

The process at a glance

Eight steps toward your next chapter.

Global Recruitment & Logistics describes a decade of experience supporting international healthcare professionals. Each candidate’s route is individual, and the specific sequence and timeline can vary.

Getting started

The application is a conversation, not a commitment.

The first step is a chance for both sides to learn more. Global Recruitment & Logistics says its recruiting team takes time to understand each applicant, answer questions and explain the process clearly.

What to prepare

  • Resume or CV: current contact details, education and work history.
  • Nursing background: schools attended, credentials earned and clinical experience.
  • Licensure history: current and previous licenses and any relevant status information.
  • Proficiency exams: details of exams taken or planned, when applicable.
  • Your questions: role expectations, process steps, costs, timing and support.
Be authentic in your interview.
Talk openly about your experience, career goals and reasons for considering a U.S. opportunity. Recruiters can then discuss fit, expectations and next steps with you. Some records take time to obtain, so ask early which documents may be needed and who is responsible for gathering them.
After the interview

Read the terms. Verify the requirements.

If both sides decide to move forward, the next stages may include a written offer, document collection, credential review, state licensure steps, exam preparation, and immigration processing where applicable.

01

Offer and contract

Review all written terms, responsibilities and conditions before accepting. Ask for clarification where anything is unclear.

02

Licensure and NCLEX-RN

U.S. nursing licensure is handled by state boards. NCSBN notes that state requirements vary and boards use the NCLEX-RN for registered nurse licensure.

Check NCSBN guidance ↗

03

Preparation program

Global Recruitment & Logistics describes a six-to-seven-month NCLEX review program with costs covered. Confirm current availability, eligibility, covered expenses and terms in your written program documents.

Important: Licensure and immigration decisions are made by the relevant state boards and government authorities. The National Council of State Boards of Nursing provides state-specific guidance for internationally educated nurses. A recruiter cannot guarantee an exam result, license, visa, job placement or processing timeline.
A people-first process

Take the time you need to make an informed decision.

Bridget Wills, identified by Global Recruitment & Logistics as Senior Manager of Global Development, emphasizes a careful and personal approach: applicants should receive thoughtful attention, clear answers and space to understand the commitment involved.

Her advice to nurses considering the move is to stay open to learning, welcome new experiences and make the most of professional and personal growth along the way.
