Document Planning

Organize your records and keep track of what you need as you prepare for recruitment and relocation.

A practical checklist

Plan ahead and stay organized.

Preparing your documents early can help make your recruitment and relocation journey more organized and less stressful. The records you need may vary depending on your professional background, destination, employer requirements and immigration process.

01 · Checklist

Identify the Documents You May Need

Start by creating a checklist of important records, which may include:

  • Personal identification: A valid passport, birth certificate and other identification documents, as required.
  • Education and training: Academic certificates, transcripts, professional training records and other relevant qualifications.
  • Professional licensing: Current nursing or healthcare licenses, registration records and verification documents, where applicable.
  • Employment history: An updated CV or résumé, employment letters and records of relevant work experience.
  • References: Contact details for professional referees and reference letters, if requested.
  • Immigration and travel records: Required application forms, supporting documents, approvals and travel information, depending on your circumstances.
  • Family documents: Marriage certificates, birth certificates for dependents and other supporting records, if family members will accompany you.
02 · Sources

Know Where Each Document Comes From

Identify the organization or person responsible for issuing each record. For example, academic documents may come from your school or university, professional licensing records from the relevant licensing authority, and employment letters from previous employers. Immigration documents should be obtained through the appropriate official channels.

03 · Ownership

Assign Responsibility

For every item on your checklist, indicate whether you are responsible for obtaining it, whether an employer or recruitment coordinator will assist, or whether another institution must issue or verify it. Confirm responsibilities early so that important documents are not overlooked.

04 · Timing

Estimate How Long Each Document May Take

Processing times can vary considerably. Some documents may be available immediately, while official transcripts, license verification, background checks or immigration-related records may take several weeks or longer. Contact the issuing organization for current estimates and allow extra time for unexpected delays.

05 · Tracking

Track Progress and Keep Records Organized

Maintain a simple document tracker showing:

  • The document required
  • Where it must be obtained
  • Who is responsible for obtaining it
  • The estimated processing time
  • The date requested and expected completion date
  • Whether it has been received, verified or submitted

Keep secure copies of important records and check whether certified copies, official translations or specific formats are required before submitting anything.

How We Can Support You

Our team can help you understand the document-planning steps relevant to your recruitment journey, clarify responsibilities and identify questions to raise with the appropriate authorities or employer. Requirements vary by role and individual circumstances, so always confirm the latest instructions before making submissions.
