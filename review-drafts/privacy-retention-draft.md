## Proposed website wording

### How long we keep application information

We keep an unsuccessful applicant’s application and CV for up to 12 months after the application is closed. An application with no recorded activity for six months may be closed; the 12-month period then begins. If you separately agree to be considered for future opportunities, we keep your application information for up to 12 months from that agreement. We will ask for renewed permission before keeping it longer for that purpose.

If you are hired, we move information needed for your employment to the company’s employment records and apply the retention schedule for those records. We remove duplicate application records when they are no longer needed.

We may retain information longer when required by law or when it must be preserved for a complaint, investigation, or legal matter. When the applicable retention period ends, we delete the application record and any uploaded CV from active database and file-storage systems. **[Confirm the provider backup-retention period and replace this note with accurate wording about when backup copies expire.]**

Application notification emails sent to company inboxes contain limited information such as your name, profession, desired role, submission date, and application ID; they do not include your CV or full application. Those messages are subject to the company email-retention process and will be deleted when no longer needed. **[Confirm the mailbox and email-provider retention schedule.]**

To ask to access, correct, or delete your application information, contact admin1@glblogistics.org or mkabeireho@glblogistics.org. We may need to retain some information to meet legal obligations or preserve it for a complaint or legal matter. We will verify requests and explain if we cannot delete particular information.

## Legal review note

For covered U.S. private employers, EEOC guidance generally describes a one-year retention period for application and other personnel records, measured from when the record was made or the personnel action, whichever is later; charge-related records may need to be kept until the matter is resolved. This is not a universal schedule for every recruiter, client, contract, or jurisdiction. See the [EEOC recordkeeping summary](https://www.eeoc.gov/employers/summary-selected-recordkeeping-obligations-29-cfr-part-1602).

If the company obtains consumer background reports, the FTC describes additional notice and authorization steps and secure disposal obligations. The current application checkbox should not be treated as the complete background-check process. See [FTC background-check guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/background-checks-what-employers-need-know).

If the company recruits in other regions, confirm local requirements before adopting this U.S.-focused proposal. For example, the GDPR includes a storage-limitation principle requiring identifiable personal data not be kept longer than necessary for its purposes. See the [official GDPR text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679).
